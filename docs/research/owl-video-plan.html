Making the OWL video with Claude

The first OWL demo video exists: 1 minute 49 seconds, 1080p, voice and captions, rendered by one command on base OWL with fictional data. It is correct and clear, but it looks like a careful screen recording. This note covers what a senior video editor would add, and which Claude Code skills help us add it.

Where the video stands

The pipeline is in tools/demo-video/: Playwright drives the real app and captures frames, Kokoro (a free local voice) reads the narration, FFmpeg joins them. Eleven scenes follow docs/demo-scenarios.md. No paid tool and no outside code is used.

What a senior editor works on

Seven areas, in the order an editor usually handles them. The numbers are common working ranges, not fixed rules.

1. Story

  • One promise per video.
  • Hook in the first 5 seconds: the problem, not the logo.
  • Show only the 2 or 3 interactions that prove the promise.
  • End with one clear next step.

2. Pacing

  • Cut every second where nothing happens.
  • Speed up waits; never show a spinner at normal speed.
  • Hold text long enough to read it twice.
  • Let the voice start slightly before or after the picture changes, so cuts feel smooth.

3. Motion

  • Nothing moves at constant speed. Entering things slow down; leaving things speed up.
  • Small interface moves last 150 to 300 ms.
  • Items in a list arrive 40 to 80 ms apart.
  • One thing moves at a time. Motion pulls the eye, so use it only where the viewer should look.

4. Camera

  • Zoom in to the area of action, then back out.
  • Smooth pointer paths; no jitter.
  • Highlight or dim to direct attention.
  • Crop out anything messy or irrelevant.

5. Graphics and type

  • One type scale and one colour set, taken from the brand.
  • Callout labels that say why a thing matters.
  • Captions of at most two lines, clear of the action.
  • Consistent title and section cards.

6. Sound

  • Voice is the lead. Level it to a standard loudness.
  • Quiet music under the voice, lowered further while it speaks.
  • Soft sounds on clicks and transitions.
  • No sudden silence between scenes.

7. Finish

  • Watch it once with sound off and once with eyes closed.
  • Export 1080p H.264, plus the caption file.
  • A thumbnail frame that states the promise.
  • Keep it between 60 and 120 seconds.

Suggested order of improvements

  1. Sound first. Level the voice, add a quiet music track that lowers under speech, add soft click and transition sounds from soundcn. FFmpeg can do all three, and the recorder already knows the exact time of every click. This is the largest change in how finished the video feels.
  2. Zoom on the action. Scale the page toward the spotlit element and back, with eased motion.
  3. Clean transitions. Hide loading states behind a short crossfade between tabs.
  4. Callout labels. A short label next to each spotlight that says why it matters.
  5. Stronger opening and ending. Start on the problem; end with a next step.
  6. Our own skill. Done: the owl-demo-video skill below holds this checklist, so every render follows it.

Steps 2 to 4 are where the GSAP skills and a motion craft skill help most.

The command

The editor's brief is saved in the OWL repo as a project skill, .claude/skills/owl-demo-video/SKILL.md. In Claude Code, opened in the OWL folder, one command starts the work. Anything typed after it is the brief for that render.

  • /owl-demo-videoImprove the current video by the next unfinished craft item, then render and verify it.
  • /owl-demo-video add music and click sounds, level the voiceOne focused pass.
  • /owl-demo-video 60 second cut for a sales email, focus on silent changesA new cut for a stated audience and length.

Each run follows the same loop: read the last render, fix the script, draft without voice, render, verify with numbers, and report what was and was not checked. It will not commit, install a skill or record a scene that writes data without a yes.

# OWL demo video

You are the senior video editor and motion designer for the Kwartz OWL demo video.
The video is rendered by code, never recorded or edited by hand: Playwright drives the
real app and captures frames, Kokoro reads the narration, FFmpeg joins them. Your job
is the editor's job: decide what the viewer must understand, cut everything else, and
make each second look and sound finished.

Anything the user typed after the command is the brief for this render (audience,
length, focus, a scene to add or cut). With no brief, improve the existing video by
the next unfinished item in "Craft standard" and render it.

## Fixed rules

- **Base OWL only.** Record the synthetic demo world (tenant Acme Aero) on the local
  stack. No real tenant's data, names or screens may appear. After each
  render confirm that no scene's page text contains a real customer's name.
- **Say on screen that the data is fictional** (the end card does).
- **Free and clean licences only.** Playwright, FFmpeg, Kokoro, GSAP, HyperFrames.
  Sound effects only from CC0 sources (soundcn's CC0 sets; never its World of
  Warcraft set). No Remotion (paid company licence). Read OpenMontage for ideas but
  copy no code from it (AGPL). Music must be royalty-free or generated, with the
  source written in the README.
- **Scenes only read data.** No uploads, edits or deletes during a render, so two
  renders on one day tell the same story. A scene that writes needs the user's yes.
- **Narration avoids exact counts and dates.** The demo clock moves the data daily.
- **Steps wait for real page states**, never fixed sleeps.
- **Do not commit, push, open a pull request or touch the work tracker** unless asked in this
  conversation. Do not install a third-party skill without the user's yes.

## Craft standard

Work down this list. An item is done only when it is visible or audible in the
rendered file.

1. **Story.** One promise: OWL finds what changed in a customer's order book and
   keeps the proof. Open on the problem within 5 seconds, not on the logo. Show only
   the interactions that prove the promise. Target 90 to 120 seconds. End with one
   next step for the viewer; ask the user what it is if it is not known.
2. **Pacing.** Cut every moment where nothing happens. Speed up waits and mark them
   ("Sped up 8x"). Never show a spinner or "Loading" at normal speed. Hold any text
   long enough to read twice.
3. **Motion.** Nothing moves at constant speed: entering elements ease out, leaving
   elements ease in. Small interface moves last 150 to 300 ms, camera moves 400 to
   800 ms, list items arrive 40 to 80 ms apart. One thing moves at a time, and only
   where the viewer should look. Use one motion language for the whole video.
4. **Camera.** Zoom toward the element being discussed, hold, then return. Keep the
   pointer path smooth. Spotlight or dim to direct attention.
5. **Graphics and type.** Colours and type come from the app's own brand. Each
   spotlight gets a short callout label that says why it matters. Captions are at
   most two lines and never cover the thing being shown. Title, section and end cards
   share one design.
6. **Sound.** The voice leads: level it to about -16 LUFS. Put a quiet music bed
   under it, lowered further while the voice speaks. Add soft sounds on clicks and
   transitions, placed from the recorder's own click times. No dead silence between
   scenes.
7. **Finish.** 1920x1080, 30 fps, H.264 with AAC, plus the caption file. Captions
   match the voice. Pick one frame that states the promise as the thumbnail.

If the pipeline cannot yet do something on this list (music, zoom, transitions,
callouts), extend the pipeline: put the capability in `lib.mjs` as a helper, use it
from `scenes.mjs`, and document it in the README. Prefer the smallest change that
gets the result.

## How to work

1. **Read first.** The README, `scenes.mjs`, and the last render if one exists
   (sample frames from it with ffmpeg and look at them).
2. **Edit the script before the picture.** Write or revise each scene's `say` line.
   Plain words, short sentences, one idea per scene.
3. **Draft.** `npm run draft` (no voice) until every scene runs and reads well.
   Look at frames from every scene, including during clicks and transitions.
4. **Render.** `npm run video`.
5. **Verify, and report each result with its number:**
   - ffprobe: 1920x1080, 30 fps, H.264 and AAC, duration in the target range
   - speech starts within 0.2 s of each scene's first caption (ffmpeg silencedetect)
   - loudness near the target (ffmpeg loudnorm or volumedetect)
   - no real customer name in `out/demo.seen.json`
   - frames from every scene looked at: caption readable, spotlight and pointer on
     the intended element, no loading state on screen
6. **Report.** Where the file is, what changed since the last render, what was
   checked and what could not be. You cannot hear the audio: say so every time and
   ask the user to listen once before the video is shared.

Ask the user only for decisions that are theirs: the music track, the closing call
to action, a scene that writes data, installing a skill, committing.
Decisions needed

Which skills to install (my suggestion: the GSAP skills now, one motion craft skill after I read it). Where the music comes from: a royalty-free track you pick, or an AI-generated one (soundcn covers short effects only, not music). What the end card should ask the viewer to do.

Background: the method

The approach comes from an earlier internal demo video of another product: script, local voice, browser automation, FFmpeg, and no editing by hand. Base OWL needed less than that video did, because the demo world is already synthetic and repeatable, no sign-in is needed locally, and every tab has a staged story. The forecast-spike scenario for "OWL Intelligence" and a live-upload scene are not in the video; both need demo-world work first.
